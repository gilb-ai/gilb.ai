Interviews tell the story. Screens show the work.

Each discovery method sees part of the picture. Therblig puts the two most useful parts together.

What people say

Interviews miss the last mile.

People describe what they think they do. The copy-paste between tabs, the checks and the workarounds rarely come up.

What systems log

Process mining sees systems, not people.

Logs show transactions inside one system. They miss the manual work between systems, inboxes and spreadsheets.

What Therblig adds

Both views in one study.

Each finding links a person’s own words to observed work. Recommendations rest on evidence, not on a few senior interviews.

From conversations and clicks to a plan you can defend.

Outputs are ready for the client report and for the next step: building automations on Company Brain.

A study of work. Not surveillance.

Capture starts only after the scope, consent and data rules are agreed.

Consent

People opt in.

Participants know what is captured and when, and can pause recording. Employee representatives can review the scope where required.

Scope

Only agreed apps and time.

The study covers the applications and period you define. Personal and sensitive content is filtered out.

Output

Findings, not feeds.

Managers see the task map and opportunities, never a live view of someone’s screen. If the goal is productivity policing, Therblig is the wrong tool.

Why “Therblig”?Frank and Lillian Gilbreth broke manual work into elementary motions and called them therbligs, their name spelled almost backwards. We carry the same idea into screen work: understand the small actions first, then redesign the job.
