01 / THE DISCOVERY GAP

Interviews tell you the story. Screens show the work.

01 / WHAT PEOPLE SAY

Interviews alone miss the last mile.

People describe what they think they do. The copy-paste between tabs, the checks, and the workarounds rarely come up.

02 / WHAT SYSTEMS LOG

Process mining sees systems, not people.

Logs show transactions inside one system. They miss the manual work between systems, inboxes, and spreadsheets.

03 / WHAT THERBLIG ADDS

Both views in one study.

Each finding links a person’s own words to observed work. Your recommendations rest on evidence, not on a few senior interviews.

02 / WHAT YOU GET

04 / PRIVACY BY DESIGN

A study of work. Not surveillance.

Capture only starts after the scope, consent, and data rules are agreed.

01 / CONSENT

People opt in.

Participants know what is captured and when. They can pause recording. Employee representatives can review the scope where required.

02 / SCOPE

Only agreed apps and time.

The study covers the applications and period you define. Personal and sensitive content is filtered out.

03 / OUTPUT

Findings, not feeds.

Managers see the task map and opportunities, never a live camera on someone’s screen. If the goal is productivity policing, Therblig is the wrong tool.

Why “Therblig”?Frank and Lillian Gilbreth broke manual work into elementary motions and called them therbligs — their name spelled almost backwards. Gilb carries the same idea into screen work: understand the small actions first, then redesign the job.
